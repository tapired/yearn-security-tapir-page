# Grove USDS Compounder Security Review — Issue #771

## [Medium] Fixed whole-balance auctions lack a market-relative loss bound

### Details

At the final reviewed commit and its descendant, reporting or a manual kick transfers the strategy's entire GROVE balance to Auction. Auction treats the configured `10,000e18` starting price as a total-lot value, so the initial unit price is `10,000 USDS / GROVE lot` before the 30-bps-per-minute decay. The same fixed parameters must cover every lot above the 10,000-GROVE sale threshold, with no tranche cap or oracle-relative price guard.

At the 0.006-USDS/GROVE floor, a lot above approximately 1,666,666.67 GROVE starts inactive. Conversely, small lots can remain above market until the one-day auction expires; using the repository's historical 0.0078–0.0080 price range only as an example, a 10,000-GROVE lot still prices near 0.0132 after one day. Management can reconfigure and re-kick an inactive lot, so funds are not permanently stuck, but reward realization can halt or clear at an economically obsolete price. Actual deployed lot sizes and market depth were not established.

### Recommendation

Cap each auction to a depth-tested tranche, derive its start and minimum prices from a manipulation-resistant reference with an explicit maximum discount, and leave excess GROVE for later auctions. Test the full reachable lot range against the one-day duration and monitor both unfilled auctions and realized execution loss.

## [Medium] APR oracle revisions do not enforce confirmed, fresh pricing

### Details

In the reviewed `dce25e5...` oracle, large moves require confirmation before updating the cache, but `_grovePrice()` returns any complete live route first. The cache therefore does not constrain APR while a route fills. A single configured pool is permitted, and median filtering begins only when at least three pools provide fully filled first chunks.

The later, unreviewed oracle rewrite in `81afbf8...` replaces the expiring cache with a stored price that never expires. When the live route is unavailable or differs by more than 50%, that stored value drives APR indefinitely; an authorized price setter may also store any nonzero manual value while no complete live route exists. The 50% APR cap limits magnitude, and these prices affect allocation signals rather than strategy accounting or auction settlement. However, the claimed deployed oracle was not matched to either prepared runtime, so deployment and downstream allocator impact remain unresolved.

### Recommendation

Require confirmed live prices to pass the same deviation policy used for cache updates, require multiple independent usable pools, and impose a short maximum age plus conservative decay on stored/manual values. Bound manual inputs on-chain. Before use, reproduce the claimed oracle bytecode and test manipulation cost and allocation impact through the exact registered consumer.

## [Low] Late deposits can capture reward value accrued before entry

### Details

Share issuance uses the framework's last reported USDS value, while claimable GROVE, GROVE in an active auction, and returned-but-unreported USDS are excluded. If deposits are open, an allowlisted entrant—or a user whose allowed parent vault auto-allocates—can enter before recognition and receive part of the old reward profit after it unlocks.

A prepared pinned-fork PoC on the earlier revision demonstrated the mechanism: an equal 10,000-USDS late deposit captured 7.629394531249999928 USDS from a 15.258789062499999856-USDS pre-entry reward lot, or 6.865931249141758529 USDS after the default performance fee. The accounting root remains in the final target. Direct deposits default closed, capital must remain through profit unlock, and the actual parent-vault configuration was not proven, which limits severity and deployment reachability.

### Recommendation

Keep direct deposits closed and prevent parent allocation while claimable rewards, active auctions, or unreported proceeds exist, or explicitly reserve that value for the pre-entry share supply. Port the existing PoC to the final commit and run it through each claimed parent vault's actual allowlist and auto-allocation settings.

## [Low] A staking pause can block reports that hold auction proceeds

### Details

`availableDepositLimit()` returns zero when Grove staking is paused, but `_harvestAndReport()` does not apply the same condition. While the strategy is live, any idle balance above one USDS is passed to `STAKING.stake()`. Under the issue-stated behavior that pause rejects new stakes, a settled reward auction can therefore make `report()` revert, leaving profit, `lastReport`, and parent-vault reporting stale until external unpause or irreversible strategy shutdown.

The strategy-side path is exact, but the prepared evidence contains only the staking interface—not the implementation or runtime—so the pause revert remains a targeted integration hypothesis rather than independently verified deployed behavior. The issue indicates withdrawals remain available, limiting the impact to reporting and compounding liveness.

### Recommendation

When staking is paused, report idle USDS without attempting to redeploy it. Against the exact staking deployment, test report, full withdrawal, reward claim, and shutdown recovery after an auction settles during pause.

## [Low] Late auction proceeds can be orphaned after final shutdown redemption

### Details

An active auction holds GROVE outside strategy accounting. After shutdown and emergency withdrawal, the final shareholder can redeem all shares before that auction settles. A later take then sends USDS to a permanently shut-down strategy with zero external share supply and zero deposit capacity. Reporting cannot assign the value to the former shareholders, and no reviewed function rescues the underlying asset.

The repository contains a focused test that executes this sequence and asserts zero supply, a reverting replacement deposit, and the late USDS remaining in the strategy. The path affects pending reward proceeds rather than principal and is avoidable by correct offboarding order.

### Recommendation

Do not permit final debt removal or final share redemption while any reward auction is outstanding. Alternatively, account for the receivable before redemption or provide a narrowly scoped recovery mechanism that preserves the former shareholders' entitlement. Test the fix through the parent vault's debt-zeroing and revoke flows.

## [Low] APR queries can divide by zero for an empty staking pool

### Details

Both oracle revisions divide annualized reward value by the staking contract's adjusted `totalSupply()` without checking for zero. If rewards are still active, total staked supply is zero, and `_delta` is zero, `aprAfterDebtChange()` panics instead of returning a usable current APR. No prepared evidence demonstrates that state on the claimed deployment, and the failure affects a view/allocation path rather than funds accounting.

### Recommendation

Define and return a safe value—normally zero—when adjusted assets are zero, and add tests for active rewards with zero supply through both the oracle and central APR registry.

## [Informational] Deployment, roles, parent configuration, and staking behavior are unverified

### Details

The August 30 issue comment claims strategies `0xe060B80438771f13078048c3b0d930efECA6E622` and `0x47c640fDA687B7D20d50D4464e302e36D3D312Ed` and oracle `0xae71A2F0089fa802a55FcB2152BEF87A2C9Aadf9`. The local broadcast records the first strategy but a different oracle, `0xed85bb86bdef06ef7312ed1bd3f80a4c2f957f56`, and contains neither the second strategy nor the claimed oracle. No prepared runtime-bytecode comparison or readback proves strategy management, keeper, emergency admin, deposit access, auction configuration, oracle setters/pools, central registration, or parent-vault debt and queue settings. The fixed staking contract's implementation, administrators, caps, pause behavior, and exact withdrawal semantics are likewise absent.

Consequently, the reviewed source findings and fixes cannot be attributed to all claimed deployments, and deployed bytecode or external behavior was not verified.

### Recommendation

Reproduce and compare runtime bytecode using exact compiler metadata for every claimed address. Record all strategy, auction, oracle, registry, and parent-vault roles and configuration, then pin the staking implementation and test deposits, full and partial withdrawals, pause, reward claims, caps, and report-time restaking at a fixed block before release clearance.
