# Security Review — Flex Looper v2 Comment Cutover

## [Medium] Hard-coded v1 helper is incompatible with the pinned Flex v2 ABI

### Details

The final strategy pins Flex v2 but hard-codes the earlier helper at `0xA411…EbD6`. Lever-up reaches this helper through `_lever()` → `_maxBorrowAmount()`. Flex v2 inserted `last_debt_increase_time` into the returned `Trove` structure, making the old helper’s decoder incompatible.

The repository’s own v2 tests acknowledge this mismatch and pass only after using `vm.etch` to replace the hard-coded address with newly compiled v2 helper code. The deployment script separately instructs operators to update the constant for v2. With `ALLOW_REDEMPTION == true`, the mismatch can make keeper tends revert instead of deploying idle deposits.

### Recommendation

Use a constructor-supplied immutable v2 helper and validate its code hash or version. Remove the test-only `vm.etch`, then prove that the actual configured helper supports a complete redemption-enabled lever-up against the pinned Flex revision.

## [Medium] Management can manufacture a loss and capture existing principal

### Details

Management can apply any `reportBuffer` below 10,000 bps to collateral accounting, disable the health check for each report, and control the deposit allowlist. It can therefore report an artificial loss, admit a controlled depositor at the depressed share price, restore the buffer, report the apparent recovery, and redeem part of the original cohort’s principal.

For example, a 3x position with collateral worth 300, debt of 200, and 100 shares has 100 real assets. A 3,000-bps buffer reports only 10 assets. A 10-asset deposit then mints 100 shares; restoring truthful accounting makes those shares worth 55 assets, transferring 45 from existing holders.

### Recommendation

Do not apply a management-controlled discount to reported asset value. Use conservatism only for allocation or deposit limits, or make any accounting adjustment immutable and tightly bounded. Block deposits across unexpected loss states and add a two-cohort loss, deposit, restoration, and redemption regression test.

## [Medium] Keeper-controlled rate changes can impose fees and extreme interest

### Details

`adjustInterestRate()` is available to the keeper with an arbitrary new rate and `maxUpfrontFee`. The pinned Flex implementation accepts rates from 0.1% through 250%, adds an upfront fee to Trove debt for premature adjustments, and has no strategy-specific rate or fee policy.

A compromised keeper can alternate rates to add fees until the collateral-ratio check prevents further calls, or leave the position at 250% interest so its remaining equity rapidly erodes toward liquidation. The keeper does not need management control or direct token custody.

### Recommendation

Restrict discretionary rate changes to management, or enforce governance-approved minimum and maximum rates, maximum changes, fee limits, and cooldowns in the strategy. Test repeated premature changes at the intended leverage and prove keeper actions cannot materially increase debt or liquidation risk.

## [Medium] Maintenance can create new debt after shutdown

### Details

Tokenized Strategy leaves `tend()` and `report()` callable after shutdown. The strategy’s `_tend()` calls `_lever()` without checking shutdown, allowing idle assets to be converted, supplied, and accompanied by new Flex debt. A report can also enter `_lever()` when leverage exceeds its maximum and, with sufficient idle assets, recompute a target requiring additional debt.

This lets compromised or stale keeper automation re-deploy recovered liquidity, restart the Flex repayment cooldown, and reintroduce liquidation exposure after an irreversible shutdown. A completed emergency close prevents reopening, but the unsafe window remains while the Trove is active.

### Recommendation

After shutdown, prohibit every path that supplies collateral, increases debt, or converts idle assets for deployment. Reports and tends should only value assets, repay debt, and free capital. Test both maintenance entry points before and after emergency closure.

## [Medium] Immutable governance can assign an exchange that takes loose principal

### Details

The immutable `GOVERNANCE` address can replace the exchange and automatically grant it unlimited asset and collateral allowances. In factory deployments this authority is fixed to the factory’s management at creation and survives later Tokenized Strategy management transfers.

A compromised current or former governance address can install an attacker-controlled exchange, which can then use `transferFrom()` outside a strategy conversion to take loose principal. Fresh deposits are particularly exposed because `_deployFunds()` intentionally leaves them idle until a keeper tends.

### Recommendation

Make exchange authority rotate with current governance and use a delayed two-step venue change. Prefer exact per-conversion approvals that are cleared afterward, and validate approved exchange code hashes. Test that former governance and an installed venue cannot transfer loose strategy balances independently.

## [Low] `manualFullUnwind()` cannot close an active Flex Trove

### Details

`manualFullUnwind()` sets target leverage to zero and runs the generic deleverage path. That path flash-borrows the full debt, but Flex ordinary repayment stops at `MIN_DEBT`; it then attempts to remove all collateral while minimum debt remains, causing the collateral-ratio check to revert.

The repository explicitly skips this test for Flex. Shutdown followed by `emergencyWithdraw()` uses the correct `close_trove` or `close_zombie_trove` path, but the separately advertised full-unwind function is unusable for an active debt position.

### Recommendation

Override `manualFullUnwind()` to use the protocol-specific full-close flow and convert the released collateral safely. Align exact-final withdrawal limits with the minimum-debt constraint, and test active, zombie, and final-share exits.

## [Informational] Cooldown risk at the intended leverage remains unverified

### Details

The cutover correctly stops advertising a tend that Flex would reject during its repayment cooldown. Flex nevertheless prevents repayment and both close paths until strictly after that interval, while liquidation remains available; the factory permits cooldowns of up to one hour.

Whether this creates material loss depends on the final target leverage, maximum leverage, cooldown, oracle behavior, and collateral volatility. The narrow 10x configuration in the checked-in script belongs to an earlier deployment lineage and was not proven for final code.

### Recommendation

Stress the exact proposed market through adverse oracle moves during the complete cooldown. Demonstrate sufficient distance from MCR and an executable add-collateral rescue, and operate an independent LTV alert because `tendTrigger()` intentionally remains false during this interval.

## [Informational] Final deployment and runtime composition are not established

### Details

No prepared artifact proves deployment of the final `4c269b3` source or its pinned dependencies. The available strategy broadcast predates the v2 branch, while the final deployment script still warns that its market and helper values are for v1.

Final runtime bytecode, constructor values, Flex market, helper, exchange, oracle, APR registration, roles, and concrete parent Vault configuration are therefore unverified. The findings above describe the reviewed source and must not be interpreted as claims about deployed bytecode or live external behavior.

### Recommendation

Reproduce final creation and runtime bytecode from the pinned compiler and dependencies. Preserve all immutable values, external code hashes, roles, delays, parent Vault revision and queue, debt limits, allowlists, hooks, and APR configuration, then execute deposit, tend, report, cooldown, liquidation, withdrawal, shutdown, and emergency-close tests against that exact composition.
